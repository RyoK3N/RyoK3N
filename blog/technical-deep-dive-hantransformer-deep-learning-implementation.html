The project "HANTransformer" addresses the technical challenge of effectively handling long and structured text data, such as documents, for classification tasks. Traditional models often struggle with capturing the hierarchical structure and long-range dependencies in such data, leading to suboptimal performance. To overcome these limitations, the project leverages a Hierarchical Attention Network (HAN) combined with a Transformer architecture. The HAN captures the document structure at multiple levels, while the Transformer excels in capturing long-range dependencies through self-attention mechanisms. Key technical decisions include the integration of bidirectional attention within the HAN to enhance the representation of document segments and the use of Transformer layers to process these segments effectively. These choices aim to improve the model's ability to understand and classify documents accurately.

The engineering approach and key technical decisions are reflected in the design of the HANTransformer model, which involves a multi-stage processing pipeline. The first stage involves segmenting the documents into sentences and capturing their local context using HAN, followed by a second stage that uses Transformer layers to aggregate and refine these contexts. The implementation emphasizes modularity and reusability, with separate code components for data preprocessing, model architecture definition, and training and evaluation processes. This modular design facilitates easier maintenance and further experimentation with different model configurations.

By examining the actual code, readers will gain insights into the implementation of a sophisticated deep learning model that combines the strengths of HAN and Transformer architectures. ### Core Algorithms

The core of the HANTransformer project involves the integration of hierarchical attention networks (HAN) and transformers. The hierarchical attention network is designed to capture the hierarchical structure of documents, where sentences are processed hierarchically, and the transformer model is used to capture long-range dependencies and interactions between different parts of the document.

The HAN model consists of two stages: sentence-level and document-level. At the sentence-level, the model uses a bi-directional LSTM to process each sentence and capture its internal structure. Each sentence is then assigned an attention score based on its relevance to the document, which is computed using a self-attention mechanism. The document-level attention mechanism then aggregates the sentence representations, weighted by their attention scores, to produce a document-level representation.

The transformer model is integrated to further enhance the document representation by capturing long-range dependencies and interactions between sentences. The transformer model consists of a series of self-attention layers, followed by feed-forward neural networks, which are applied across all sentences in the document. This dual-layer architecture ensures that the model can effectively capture both the local and global structures of the document.
